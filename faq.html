Frequently Asked Questions

Welcome to the ROJA FAQ page. Here you'll find answers to the most common questions across key areas of our platform.

General Overview

Roja is a white-labeled Earned Wage Access (EWA) technology platform that enables organizations to offer EWA experiences under their own brand. Roja provides the technology and operational tooling required to launch and manage EWA programs while allowing partners to retain full control over branding, customer relationships, program structure, and operational decisions.

No.
Roja does not provide funds and does not operate a financial product.
Roja supplies technology, workflows, and integrations that support EWA programs. All funding arrangements, program economics, and operational decisions are determined and managed by the partner.

No.
Roja is not a lender and does not originate, underwrite, or offer financial products.
Roja functions solely as a technology provider supporting EWA programs through software, integrations, and configurable workflows.

Program Features & Technology

Roja is designed to support a range of EWA structures, including:
  • Earned Wage Access (EWA)
  • No-cost access models
Programs are configurable to align with each partner's operating model, policies, and applicable requirements.

Roja is fully white-labeled.
This means:
  • The experience is branded entirely as your own
  • You control naming, messaging, and disclosures
  • End users engage directly with your brand
  • Roja remains unseen as the underlying technology layer
Roja does not appear in user-facing branding or communications unless explicitly chosen by the partner.

Yes.
Roja is designed to integrate with existing systems and providers, including:
  • Payroll or income data sources
  • Bank and account verification tools
  • Internal servicing, CRM, or management systems

Operations & Control

The partner does.
Organizations deploying Roja retain full ownership of the customer relationship, including branding, communications, support, and program management. Roja does not market to, onboard, or directly communicate with end users.

Roja provides configurable eligibility and access logic defined by the partner.
Eligibility parameters may include, but are not limited to:
  • Income or payroll data
  • Bank account activity
  • Program rules established by the partner
All eligibility criteria and thresholds are controlled by the organization using the platform.

No.
Roja does not make independent decisions regarding access amounts, limits, or frequency.
The platform executes rules and logic configured by the partner based on their defined program structure.

Roja provides tools that support reconciliation and repayment workflows based on partner-defined program structures.
Timing, methods, and logic are fully configurable and controlled by the partner to align with their operational model.

Compliance & Legal

Roja is built with configurability, transparency, and audit readiness in mind.
The platform enables partners to:
  • Configure disclosures and user flows
  • Define program rules and eligibility logic
  • Maintain reporting and activity records
  • Support internal reviews and audits
Each partner remains responsible for ensuring their EWA program complies with applicable laws, regulations, and internal policies.

No.
Roja does not provide legal or regulatory advice.
The platform provides technical capabilities that partners can configure to align with their compliance approach and professional guidance.

Audience & Implementation

Roja is designed for organizations that want to offer EWA programs under their own brand, including:
  • Financial service providers
  • Licensed operators
  • Employers and employer platforms
  • Consumer finance or workforce platforms
The platform is intended for organizations seeking flexibility, control, and faster deployment without building technology internally.

Because Roja is a turnkey platform, partners can typically deploy programs faster than building an internal solution.
Deployment timelines depend on factors such as integrations, configuration complexity, branding requirements, and internal readiness.
